11. Plan a job and hand the crew one parameter sheet

For: whoever prepares a survey - a processor, a planner, a party chief.
Assumes: nothing beyond Part I. No seismic file is opened; this is planning, before anything is recorded.

The job. The field crew needs one file that says what to lay out and how to record it: the job, the geometry, the source, the receivers, the recording settings, and where the lines are. The Parameters Sheet tab (the last icon on the rail) walks you through it and saves a single Excel workbook. Every value in it is planned: typed, picked from a list or derived from what you typed, never measured.

1. Start with the job type

Pick the Job type first. It fills the recommended values for that kind of job - the intervals, sample rate, record length, low cut, source, stack, geophone frequency and type, spread type and channel count - and shows the recommended range under each field. Everything stays editable, and you can always type a value that is not in a list. You can also add your company logo (PNG or JPEG); it goes at the right of the sheet title and on the map. Nothing on this tab is kept when the app closes: each launch starts with an empty sheet, so save the workbook before you quit.

2. Lines, source, receivers, recording

On Geometry, set the intervals and channels and add each line with its name and length; the number of lines, stations, shots and the nominal fold are worked out for you. The Source, Receiver and Recording steps follow. Nothing is mandatory: fill in what you know, skip any step, jump to any step in any order. A Hint points at a likely mistake (a high cut above Nyquist, typed total shots that do not match the plan, an MASW array shorter than twice the target depth, SH work planned with vertical geophones) but never stops you. The sheet you save lists only what was filled in.

3. The coordinates

Paste the points, or load a TXT, CSV, KML or GeoJSON file. SeisConv works out the delimiter, the columns, whether the numbers are degrees or metres, and proposes a coordinate system - and shows you what it found. Press Confirm when it is right, or correct it with the CRS search. When the numbers cannot tell (which UTM zone, which column is latitude) it asks. If the point names describe different lines from the Geometry step, it says so and offers to take the line names and lengths from the coordinates. No area yet? Tick No area yet; the sheet then says "Survey area: not provided".

4. Review and save

The last step shows everything on one page with an Edit link per step, and the area map exactly as it will go into the workbook. Press Save parameter sheet (XLSX)....

The workbook has a one-page Parameters sheet (label, value, unit; numbers are real numbers and dates real dates), an Area sheet with every point's Easting, Northing, latitude and longitude, a Map sheet with the area map, and an About sheet. The map basemap is downloaded only when you save (or ask for a preview); with no connection the sheet is still saved, the map drawn on grey with a note, and the coordinates are exact.
